# Phase 3 differentiation test

These are blunt answers to ten questions about whether Amperes is worth using. Where an answer is weak, it says so.
We have run no customer interviews and seen no customer data. The wedge under test is the **read-only usable-data
yield audit**, with incident linking as the supporting capability ([COMPETITIVE_GAP.md](COMPETITIVE_GAP.md)).

## 1. What is different from logs, dashboards and scripts?

None of the individual pieces is new. What is different is the combination:
- **Exactly one status per episode,** with a fixed precedence order (`amperes/quality.py`).
- **Operational events attached to episodes,** so a recording error makes an episode `infrastructure_error`
  whatever its task outcome (`amperes/ingest/pipeline.py` `link_events`).
- **A failed task with valid data counts as usable.**
- **Each lost episode is attributed exactly once,** with episode-hours (`ops.yield_loss_attribution`).
- **Missing data is reported as "not computable"** rather than zero.
- **Every report section is labelled with its evidence class.**

A dashboard can show counts. Amperes shows where each lost episode went and on what evidence.

**Weak point:** a competent team can build the same combination itself (see Q6).

## 2. What is the most painful manual step?

**Unknown.** No customer has told us. Our hypothesis is the join between operational events or tickets and
episodes, needed to decide whether an episode is usable and why.

The prototype now makes that join explicit. On the synthetic package, 51 lost episodes were attributed as follows
[RECOMP]:

| Attributed to | Episodes | Episode-hours | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| `event data_recording_failure:CAM_FRAME_DROP` | 43 | 0.441 | wc-B |
| `finding C005_missing_outcome` | 8 | 0.063 | wc-C |

**This is unvalidated on real data.** The incidents themselves are still separate: `C004_truncated` and
`CAM_FRAME_DROP` are two incidents over the same 43 episodes [PIL]. The attribution table is what joins them.

## 3. What is the hardest capability to build reliably?

**Precise findings on someone else's data.**
- Rules check only what the stream declarations state, and the declarations are easy to get wrong.
- Even on our own simulator, two declarations had to be corrected after the first evaluation run:
  - the finger-gap valid range (`amperes/sim/runner.py` comment);
  - truncation checks, which now ignore action streams (`amperes/quality.py` `C004_truncated` comment).
- `--rules-config` makes thresholds tunable. It does not make them correct.

**Second hardest: independent outcome verification on real data.** Without it, strict verified yield is 0/480
[PILj], and usable yield rests on source labels (362 `valid_success_unverified`) [PIL].

**Third hardest: attribution that is right, not just consistent.** The rule "earliest linked event first" will
blame an event whenever one is linked, even if a finding was the real reason.

## 4. Is there evidence of time saved or quality gained?

- **Time saved: none.** The workflow study instrument exists (`amperes/review.py`), but no human has run it. The
  `workflow_tasks` table in `amperes_data/amperes.db` is empty, and [PIL] reads "no workflow study recorded — time
  savings are not claimed".
- **Quality gained: only on synthetic data the same author wrote:**
  - Every detectable injected defect was caught, 4/4 for each of 15 types; 40/40 clean episodes were preserved; and
    16/16 failed tasks were kept valid [DET].
  - All 6 planted patterns surfaced [PIL vs TRUTH].
- **Reviewed precision: none.** 0 of 56 non-info findings reviewed; 397 info notes excluded [RECOMP].

## 5. Why would a team reject it?

1. "We already have scripts for this." This is likely, and the OpenAI postings say as much
   ([research/OPENAI_PUBLIC_SIGNALS.md](research/OPENAI_PUBLIC_SIGNALS.md) §C4).
2. There is no importer for their format. Only the JSONL and CSV-package adapters exist.
3. Data cannot leave their environment, or a security review blocks a trial.
4. It does not write into their ticketing system.
5. Attribution conflicts with their own root-cause analysis. It is linkage, not cause.
6. The simulator is a toy gantry, irrelevant to their robots.
7. There is no evidence on real data, and validation is by a single author.
8. **Config overhead.** Their usable definition and thresholds now go into `analysis.json` and `rules.json`, but
   someone has to agree and maintain those files.

## 6. What can be reproduced internally with a small script?

**Most of it, bluntly.**
- **A day or two:** NaN, range, monotonic-timestamp, gap, duplicate and missing-outcome checks (Pandera or SQL);
  yield and throughput counts; grouping events by code; and a first-match attribution like ours.
- **A few weeks:** the status precedence model with evidence; the "not computable" discipline; provenance;
  read-only fingerprinting; and the counterbalanced study.

What a small script will not give a team is an *externally designed* rule library and taxonomy, plus a labelled
defect corpus to test its own checker against [DET]. That is a modest advantage.

## 7. What makes it more useful after month one?

- **Rule precision accumulates.** Reviewed findings build per-rule precision on the team's data
  (`review report` → `by_rule`, `rule_noise_ranking`), and noisy rules move into `disabled_rules`.
- **Attribution history becomes a trend,** showing which causes lose the most episode-hours week over week. Nothing
  computes this trend yet.
- **Incident history accumulates,** including repeat failure after resolution (1/1 in the synthetic run [PIL]).
- **Before/after comparisons across config changes** become possible (B004: 29.9 s → 37.5 s median cycle time after
  `cfg-v1->cfg-v2` [PIL]).
- **Recurring-incident reduction** needs a longer window. It is `not_computable` today
  ([ECONOMIC_VALUE_MODEL.md](ECONOMIC_VALUE_MODEL.md)).

**Weak point:** nothing learns or adapts automatically. Rules are static until someone edits the config or the code.

## 8. What infrastructure is reusable?

- The canonical schema (`amperes/schema.py`) and the adapter interface (`amperes/ingest/base.py`).
- Versioned rules (`RULESET_VERSION` 1.0.0), with settings recorded in `import_report.rule_settings`.
- The evidence-classed report (`amperes/pilot_report.py`), with `analysis_settings` recorded.
- The append-only review store, and the study instrument (`amperes/review.py`).
- Dataset deletion (`store.delete_dataset`).
- Code-digest provenance (`amperes/provenance.py`).
- The simulator harness (`amperes/sim/`), reusable only by sim-based teams.

## 9. What is the smallest recurring paid workflow?

**A weekly read-only yield audit of one collection program's export.**
1. `shadow --rules-config` on the week's export.
2. `pilot-report --analysis-config`, which gives: yield with its definition, the yield-loss attribution table with
   episode-hours, recurring incidents, bottleneck associations, and reviewed precision.

Pricing is a proposal ([PHASE3_PILOT_OFFER.md](PHASE3_PILOT_OFFER.md) §11). The workflow needs a one-time adapter for
the customer's format.

## 10. What evidence is still missing?

- Customer interviews (scoring sheet: [PHASE3_CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md](PHASE3_CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md)).
- Any real dataset.
- Reviewed precision on real data.
- Reviewer-confirmed attribution.
- A measured workflow study.
- Willingness to pay.
- Behaviour at more than 480 episodes per run.
- Independent (non-author) validation of the rules and corpora.
